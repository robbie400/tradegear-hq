# Premium-first scope — Kitchen Equipment HQ (owner direction, 2026-10-16)

## Non-negotiable editorial positioning
**Premium equipment selected around cooking goals, not another universal kitchen gadgets directory.**
We are not reviewing every kettle, peeler, rice cooker or $50 blender merely because it has search demand. Better to deeply cover 40–60 carefully selected expensive products than 400 low-value commodity gadgets.

**Selection price filter (USD, approximate current US retail price; for editorial qualification only, not a public live price claim):**
- Preferred **$300–$1,500+** for core hero products, complete comparison clusters, canonical individual reviews.
- **$200 minimum** for ordinary *featured individual products* unless explicitly authorised for a genuinely essential comparison (e.g. under-$500 espresso guide needs a credible affordable alternative). Editorial usefulness overrides monetisation: do not omit a legitimately best-fit lower-price option from an otherwise honest comparison just because it pays less.
- Below $200: accessories inside a verified equipment *setup* (scales, tools, replacement parts) **without standalone SEO review campaigns**, unless user later decides otherwise.
- $500–$3,000 high-premium equipment may have high AOV and low conversion; validate total economics (earnings per 100 qualified sessions, affiliate clicks, product classification and real merchant availability). Avoid "expensive means best" rankings.
- Commercial equipment: separate future lane; Amazon.com may not be appropriate retail outlet, and 4.5% Kitchen standard category rate cannot be assumed. No unverified commercial endorsements or unsafe power/food-handling recommendations.

## Revenue sanity
If a product qualifies for Amazon.com's *Kitchen* **standard 4.5%** category, a $200/$500/$1,000/$1,500 qualifying purchase yields **$9/$22.50/$45/$67.50** before considerations. These are **illustrations, not estimates of expected site earnings**. Classification varies; refunds/returns, terms and qualifying sales affect result. Tracking tags must belong to owner's account and be registered for new domain. A $1,000 product with a lower conversion rate may generate less per 1,000 visitors than a $350 product.

## Target category priority
1. **Premium home espresso systems and coffee stations**: machines, integrated grinders, capable burr grinders, household vs small café requirements; central price/space/setup decision, measured keyword "best espresso machine under 500" = **1K–10K US/mo** (Google Keyword Planner, Oct 2025–Sep 2026), ad competition Medium.
2. **Stand mixers for frequent bread and baking**: dough quantity and frequency, documented capacity/duty limits, bowl/attachment system. "best stand mixer for bread dough" = **1K–10K US/mo**, ad competition High.
3. **Premium air fryer/toaster-oven combination systems**: external size, ventilation, racks, actual usable baking/tray area. "best air fryer toaster oven combo" = **1K–10K US/mo**, ad competition High. *Don't promote cheap basic air fryers as core.*
4. **Nugget ice makers and premium home bar gear**: cleaning/water, ice texture, noise, unit size. "best countertop nugget ice maker" = **1K–10K US/mo**, ad competition High.
5. **Chamber vacuum sealers and higher-end preservation**: edge vs chamber, capacity/weight, chamber size, bag running costs. "best chamber vacuum sealer for home use" = **100–1K US/mo** (ad competition High); adjacent "best vacuum sealer for meat" = **1K–10K US/mo** (ad competition High) but may involve lower-AOV edge sealers, so SERP and buyer fit must be assessed before its own page.
6. **Premium blender and processor systems**: Vitamix/Blendtec-type premium systems and multifunctional prep units; verify the **high-end buying intent** rather than defaulting to "best blender for protein shakes" (1K–10K US/mo but often price-sensitive). Premium query terms currently **UNMEASURED**.
7. **Future premium niche**: home pizza ovens, specialist cooking appliances, home freeze dryers **only with** verified search volume, Amazon marketplace/category availability, safety claims, image rights and buyer intent.
8. **Future professional / commercial**: café, micro-bakery and restaurant equipment supplier programmes; **no automatic domestic recommendation** for commercial workloads.

## Qualification checklist per product
- Current exact US model & legitimate actual product lifecycle; usable Amazon.com listing or independently approved alternative affiliate partner; category classification. No fabricated/current fixed prices or ratings.
- Real cook task, servings/batch size, frequency, maintenance, usable kitchen footprint, clearance and energy/electrical requirements. Distinguish manufacturer-published claims from independent test evidence.
- **A genuinely different reason to buy**: not merely a higher priced variant. A model should represent a specific decision fork in one of the KitchenMatch flows.
- Evidence-backed practical pros/cons, "what you'd be paying extra for" and "what you can skip". Do not promote a needless upsell.
- Photo use rights, manufacturer source, honest editorial date, source-based FAQ and alternative link.
- Real chef/barista/customer quote ONLY when a real attributable source and the necessary quotation/image reuse rights exist. House-written **Kitchen Coach says** panels are original editorial guidance, **never presented as testimony or first-hand expert testing**.

## Kitchen Coach implementation strategy
**Stage 1, $0 marginal AI cost:** static expert-style *editorial voice* tips; rules-based KitchenMatch wizard with authored branching questions and manufacturer-verified model data; responsive client-side computation and no external model calls. Can be deployed in a static Next.js site. Compare suitability, not fake live prices.
**Stage 2 (only after traction):** optional server-mediated LLM for a conversational interface, with strict daily caps, model caching, safety, cited catalogue retrieval, fallback to v1 rules, minimal data collection and consent. Don't need it for launch; it is not inherently novel by itself.
**No invented chef personas:** never claim "Chef Emily recommends" unless real expert interviewed with consent. If a fictional mascot or non-human advisor icon is used, label its content as website editorial advice rather than a verified human professional.
