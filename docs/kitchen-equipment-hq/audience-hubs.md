# Who cooks? Audience hubs, category taxonomy and SEO rules
**Core principle:** A customer may identify as a baker, espresso enthusiast and busy parent; personas are **filters and decision contexts**, not exclusive memberships or cloned product pages.

## Homepage wireframe (content only)
1. Headline: **"What do you want to cook? Find the equipment that fits."**
   Subhead: "*Tell us your kitchen, portions and budget. We'll explain what makes sense — and what you can skip.*"
   Primary CTA **"Build my Kitchen Blueprint"**. Secondary **"Browse equipment buying guides"**.
2. Cook cards: Home cooking / Baking / Espresso & coffee / Meal prep / Entertaining / Professional kitchen (each links to **one substantial guide to their workflow**; professional flagged).
3. "Start with your kitchen": three clearly explained selectable scenarios (limited counter / family meals / café or bakery). Simple user task cards, rather than unsourced endorsements.
4. "Popular buying questions": keyword-linked approved guides `best-espresso-machines-under-500`, `best-air-fryer-toaster-oven-combos`, `best-stand-mixers-for-bread-dough`, `best-vacuum-sealers-for-meat`, `best-countertop-nugget-ice-makers`, `best-blenders-for-protein-shakes`.
5. "Before you buy" insights: total espresso setup cost, mixer dough batch limits, appliance clearance vs counter space, ice maker cleaning.
6. Latest substantive reviews, trust/method disclosure ("Research-led guidance. We disclose affiliate relationships. We don't claim hands-on tests we haven't done.")
7. FAQ, brand footprint, bottom disclosure.

## Audience hubs (only if independently useful)
`/for/home-cooks/`: meal quantities, counter space, cleanup/overlap; link air fryer and blender guides and meal prep setup; unique "what can one appliance replace" chart.
`/for/home-bakers/`: baking habits, dough masses, attachments, temperatures & proofing; link mixer and vacuum preservation guides; home-vs-commercial duty checklist.
`/for/coffee-enthusiasts/`: espresso drinks, grinder matching, water and machine maintenance, milk steaming; link under-$500 espresso and beginners.
`/for/meal-preppers/`: batch size, freezer workflow, bag compatibility, cleaning; link food processor and vacuum sealer.
`/for/entertainers/`: guests per session, ice/water, host workflow, limited counter; link ice maker and coffee guides.
`/for/pro-kitchens/`: separate **professional** supplier lane, not a generic home appliance hub. Distinguish cafés, bakery/pastry, restaurants, catering. US compliance (food-contact NSF/ANSI where applicable; electrical certifications and local requirements) and power/service coverage must be researched per category. No approval to recommend commercial equipment or quote regulations without verified sources.

## Navigation design
Desktop mega-menu: **By cooking goal** / **By equipment** / **By budget & space** / **Comparisons** / **KitchenMatch**. Mobile: search, compact tabs, accessible filters. Sticky "Does it fit your kitchen?" on guides. Product pages consistently include model photos, warm brown *sourced* customer-perspective panels, full pros/cons, verdict and crosslinked alternatives.

## High-ticket strategy, honestly scoped
**Residential premium**: espresso setups, mixers, chamber vacuum sealers, counter nugget ice systems, premium high-power blenders; better potential revenue per qualifying transaction but strong publisher competition and spec/safety care.
**Commercial**: café espresso/grinders, high-capacity mixers, blast chillers, vacuum sealers and induction stations **only after** partner/supplier research and professional licensing/certification research. Some aren't normally good Amazon products, and may be shipped/installed by specialist dealers. Consider lead/referral income to approved dealers **later**; do not blend commission assumptions with Amazon's 4.5% Kitchen figure. No false promise that professional equipment qualifies for that rate.
**Accessory basket**: selection is a real *working setup*, not a pile of unrelated recommended products. Recommend the few components needed for the buyer's actual workflow.

## What counts as credible "chef voice"
- User desires expertise and connection, but no invented quotes or fictional testimonials.
- Content style: *experienced kitchen equipment buying adviser*: direct practical language, concrete scenarios, "what we would question before buying"; always disclose research-only evidence.
- Interview programme later: 3–5 real working chefs, baristas, bakers, café operators, home users who consent to attributed soundbites/short quotes; record model, context, whether tested vs observed, usage permission. Label quotes as their **opinion in that context**.
- Anonymous user feedback is still copyrighted. Default to genuinely original linked synthesis and clearly sourced paraphrase.
- Consider structured Q&A **"Ask a baker"** only after partnership, not generic AI acting as a real baker.

## Avoiding SEO doorway and content duplication
User hubs are **workflow landing pages**, not copied product roundups; a user who clicks a hub reaches the same canonical guide as Google searchers. Each long-tail target gets one primary canonical URL. Cluster near duplicates ("best small rice cooker" vs "best rice cooker for one person") into one guide unless SERPs prove different intent. Do not index KitchenMatch query strings or auto-generated user results.
